## split-box-wline-vs-google.patch  ->  20_within_10pt, 190 documents  —  DO NOT SHIP AS WRITTEN

**Corrected 13 Nov, by measurement.** This section first recorded that the patch emits
`w:line = 1.38*size` and that this "makes Word and Google agree". It does not. The two models
agree only while `lineHeight(face, size) <= 12 pt`, which is below 9.4-11.4 pt depending on the
face - so 12 pt body text, the commonest size in the corpus, is OUTSIDE it. And the split moves
every baseline down by 0.2 x box even where the pitch is right. Both corrections are measured
below. The patch is not shippable in this form.

THE FINDING. The class is REAL: 199 of its 203 documents were genuine positional failures when
this was written, and the pairing defect in the geometry checks makes it UNDER-report rather than
over-report (a table one full row out of place scores 97 and passes). The class is **190**
documents today (`DEFECT-CLASSES.md:20`, regenerated from BASELINE.md on 13 Nov, 211 documents
measured); the 199-of-203 split has not been re-measured against the 190.

THE CAUSE. The engine writes `w:line = linePt*20` with `lineRule="exact"`, modelling Word, where
`advance = w:before + w:line`. Google computes
`w:before + max((w:line/240)*lineHeight(face,size), 0.069*size)`. Measured end to end with no
Drive: a 40-line 10 pt page emits `w:before="10" w:line="230"` (asking 12.00), LibreOffice renders
pitch 12.000 - the docx is exact for Word - and Google's rule gives 11.521, short 0.479 per line.
The band is crossed at line 21, so `within_10pt` is about `min(1, 21/n)`: n=25 gives 84, n=40 gives
52, n=50 gives 42. That reproduces the recorded bucket shape. **No page with more than about 21
flowed lines at this pitch can pass whatever the document is.**

THE PATCH. `patches/split-box-wline-vs-google.patch:21` adds `HAIRLINE_TW_PER_PT = 1.38f` and `:39-41` emits `w:line = round(1.38 * run.size)` twips with
`w:before = advance - w:line/20`, where `advance = 0.2*prevBox + 0.8*thisBox + gap`. That is what
the patch does. What it buys is bounded - see the next two sections.

### The rule, corrected: exact only below `lineHeight = 12 pt`

    Word / LibreOffice   advance = w:before + w:line/20
    Google               advance = w:before + max((w:line/240)*lineHeight(face,size), 0.069*size)

`w:line = 1.38*size` makes the box cost `0.069*size` pt in Word. In Google the same box costs
`(1.38*size/240)*lineHeight = 0.00575 * size * lineHeight`, and the two are equal at exactly one
size per face:

    (w:line/240) * lineHeight == w:line/20      <=>      lineHeight(face, size) == 12 pt

`lineHeight = k(face) * size`, so the agreement holds only for `size = 12/k`. Below that size
Google's floor `0.069*size` wins and the residual is exactly zero; above it Google's box is the
larger of the two and every line over-advances by `0.069*size*(k*size/12 - 1)`.

`k` per face, from the measured `w:line=240` advances at 10 pt (`REMAINING-DEFECTS.md:98-99`):

| substitute face | lineHeight at 10 pt | k | the rule is exact at or below |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liberation Serif | 12.77 | 1.277 | **9.40 pt** |
| Helvetica | 12.23 | 1.223 | 9.81 pt |
| Times-Roman | 11.70 | 1.170 | 10.26 pt |
| Arial | 11.50 | 1.150 | 10.43 pt |
| Times New Roman | 11.45 | 1.145 | 10.48 pt |
| Courier New | 10.49 | 1.049 | **11.44 pt** |

So the regime is **below 9.4-11.4 pt depending on the substitute face**, and 12 pt - the commonest
body size in the corpus - is outside it for every face. The patch's own validation probe was 10 pt
Arial (`w:before="226" w:line="14"`, above), which is INSIDE the band, which is why it looked exact.

Residual per line on Arial, computed from the measured model (k = 1.15, from the recorded 0.9583 /
1.3417 / 1.9167 ratios of the asked advance at 10 / 14 / 20 pt, `REMAINING-DEFECTS.md:92-93`),
Google minus Word:

| size | `w:line` emitted | Google's box | Word's box | residual per line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 pt | 11 tw | 0.4232 | 0.5520 | 0 (Google on its floor) |
| 10 pt | 14 tw | 0.6612 | 0.6900 | 0 (Google on its floor) |
| 10.43 pt | 14 tw | 0.7193 | 0.7197 | 0 (the boundary) |
| **12 pt** | 17 tw | 0.9522 | 0.8280 | **+0.124 pt** |
| 14 pt | 19 tw | 1.2960 | 0.9660 | +0.330 pt |
| **20 pt** | 28 tw | 2.6450 | 1.3800 | **+1.265 pt** |

The 12 pt and 20 pt rows reproduce the recorded end-to-end measurement exactly (+0.124 and +1.265
pt per line), which is what identifies this as the model that was measured rather than a
re-derivation of it. A page of 40 twelve-point lines therefore over-advances 4.96 pt - the 10 pt
result that made the patch look exact does not survive the size the corpus actually uses.

### The rule that IS exact at every size and every face

Google's box term is `(w:line/240) * k * size`. Make `w:line` a CONSTANT of at most 12 twips and
that term is at most `0.05 * k * size`; every face measured here has `k <= 1.277`, so
`0.05*k <= 0.0639 < 0.069` and Google's floor wins at EVERY size and EVERY face. The advance then
depends on nothing but the size:

    w:line = 12 (twips, constant)   w:before = advance - 0.069*size
    Google advance = w:before + 0.069*size = advance        exact, any face, any size

No face table, no size table, no regime. `12` is the largest constant that works: the inequality to
satisfy is `w:line/240 * k <= 0.069`, i.e. `w:line <= 16.56/k`, and the worst measured face
(k = 1.277) allows 12.97 - so 12 twips with margin. Anything larger re-enters the regime where the
answer depends on the substitute face, which is the defect this patch exists to remove.

The price is Word's, not Google's: Word advances `w:before + w:line/20`, so it runs short by
`0.069*size - w:line/20` = **0.228 pt per line at 12 pt**. That is the same class of cost the
hairline carries (0.65 pt/line, below), and it is why this is a Google-first rule rather than a
both-engines rule: **Google exact everywhere, Word 0.228 pt/line short at 12 pt.** Choose the engine
you are being measured on and say which one in the patch.

### It drops EVERY baseline by 0.2 x box - so nobody ships it

LibreOffice's pitch is unchanged. That part of the old note is right and is re-measured below. But a
renderer places the baseline at 0.8 of the EXACT-LINE BOX (`LINE_BASE = 0.8f`, `DocxWriter.java:93`;
probed over nine box heights and four faces, `BATCH-LOG.md:239-241`), and the split shrinks the box
to a hairline while moving the model's lead into `w:before`. The baseline of a line is
`w:before + 0.8*box` from the line above it:

    unsplit   w:before + 0.8*box      = advance - 0.2*box
    split     w:before + 0.8*hairline = advance - 0.2*hairline

so the pitch is right and **every baseline sits 0.2 x box lower than the model places it**. A page
of 12 pt body text is ~2.2 pt low from the first line to the last.

Measured. 12 pt Arial, zero margins, `w:lineRule="exact"`, four lines, LibreOffice 7.4.7.2 to PDF,
baselines read as PyMuPDF span `origin_y` (probe: `/scratch/work/probe/mk.py`):

| construct | `w:before` | `w:line` | baselines, pt from page top | pitch | first baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| model box (shipped form) | 0 | 240 tw = 12.00 pt | 9.601, 21.601, 33.601, 45.601 | 12.000 | 0.8 x 12.00 = 9.60 |
| split, `1.38*size` | 223 tw | 17 tw = 0.85 pt | 11.801, 23.801, 35.801, 47.801 | 12.000 | 11.80 (**+2.20**) |
| split, constant 12 tw | 228 tw | 12 tw = 0.60 pt | 11.901, 23.901, 35.901, 47.901 | 12.000 | 11.90 (**+2.30**) |

Pitch identical to the twip in all three - the split keeps the pitch, as claimed. Every baseline
2.20-2.30 pt lower against `0.2 x 12.00 = 2.40`; the difference is the hairline itself, the shift
being `0.2 x (box - hairline)`.

On the corpus, through Drive: **`300_prescription_rx` 55/58 -> 53/58**, with `18_median_dy` at
**-4.8** and check `30` (`30_ink_where_blank_was`) newly failing - exactly two checks lost, which is
the 55 -> 53. A uniform baseline shift is precisely what makes `18_median_dy` fire and ink land in a
band the page left blank, so the corpus result is the mechanism, not a coincidence.

SIGN, unresolved: `18_median_dy` is `render - source` (`verify_google.py:1128`), so -4.8 reads as
the render sitting 4.8 pt ABOVE the source, while the probe above puts the flow BELOW it. The
magnitude and the fact of a uniform shift agree; the direction does not, and nobody has resolved it.
Do not quote a direction until someone does.

### What to watch, corrected

The old prediction was that `44_header_footer`, `59_shape_anchor`, `41_block_spacing`,
`01_page_count` and `24_page_breaks` must NOT move "because LibreOffice's pitch is unchanged". The
premise is right and the conclusion is wrong: pitch is not baseline. Because every baseline moves
0.2 x box, those band-crossing checks are the ones that WILL move, and their moving is the evidence
that the baseline dropped - the same prediction already recorded for the hairline
(`HYPOTHESES.md:104`: "44_header_footer, 27_text_off_page and 41_block_spacing are the checks that
move while every pitch check passes"). Treat a document whose score holds but whose
`44_header_footer` / `27_text_off_page` / `30_ink_where_blank_was` / `41_block_spacing` newly fails
as a confirmed baseline shift, not as a rounding error.

WHY NOT hairline-flow. It also makes the verifier pass, and it costs Word 0.65 pt per line:
LibreOffice pitch on that patched docx is 11.351, not 12.000. Do not ship it. The same objection
applies to the constant-`w:line` rule above, at 0.228 pt/line instead of 0.65: it is the cheaper of
the two, not a free one.

## right-margin-from-source.patch  ->  47_margins, and why the class was never 203

THE FINDING. `47_margins` was failing 182 of 187 documents because of a lone space at x=0, fixed on
13 Nov in 57f521d. Behind that, one real engine residual: **the .docx carries no right margin at
all.** `sectPrXml` writes `w:pgMar right="0"` and `flowFromBox` sets `w:ind w:left=b.x` with
`rightTw = 0`, so a left-aligned paragraph has no right constraint and its text area is the whole
595 pt paper where the source's column ended at 539.

MECHANISM PROVED, not attributed. A docx built exactly that way rendered at x1 = 591.2 with
`w:ind right=0` and wrapped at 532.9 with 56 pt. 57 documents overrun the right edge and 52 of them
pass 45_char_accuracy, so the text is not garbled - it is unwrapped. How many of the 57 this
explains is NOT measured; that needs the corpus.

THE PATCH. One Ctx field, one per-page max of body-line right edges (it can only permit, never pull
in), one initial value, cell and paper-centred cases untouched. UNBUILT - there was no JDK when it
was written.

ACCEPT ON: "x1 <= source+12 AND 01/03 unchanged". The patch trades an overrun for a wrap (the probe
went 2 lines to 3), so "47_margins passes" alone is not the criterion.

## rotated-shape-dropped.patch  ->  59_shape_anchor, 121 documents

THE FINDING. `PdfToWord.java:4231` — `if (ctmRotated()) return;` inside `addShape(float[]...)`
discards every preset shape under a rotated CTM, and `ctmRotated()` (`:4153`) is true for 90 and 180
degrees, not just the 15 the comment describes. `emitStroke` (`:4385`) sends a rect or an ellipse to
`addShape(..., kind)` at `:4406` and it is dropped at `:4231`; `emitFill` (`:4266`) has the same gap.
The freeform escape exists but only for the ellipse (`:4399`, `if (ellipse && ctmRotated())`).

A `re` under a 90 or 180 degree matrix is still axis-aligned, so it takes the rectangle branch and
vanishes. The frame of a quarter-turn stamp reaches the .docx as nothing while its text is placed
correctly - which is exactly what 59 calls `lost` and what 18/19 cannot see.

CORPUS SIGNATURE. `03_stamp_vertical_90deg` 80 -> 9, `04_stamp_180deg_upside_down` 32 -> 2, and 34
of the 121 failing rows read `render=0` with `source>0`.

THE PATCH. `appendRectangle` (`:4471-4474`) already records the transformed corners, so it is two
lines, both guarded by `ctmRotated()` so no currently-correct page changes. UNCOMPILED - no JDK when
it was written.

ACCEPT ON: 03/04 stamp documents move, and no document gains a 32/33/36 failure.
